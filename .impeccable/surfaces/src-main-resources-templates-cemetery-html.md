# Surface: cemetery.html

Mode: Experience. Audience: DEV challenge readers and judges first (GIF, published link), then developers opening the cemetery of their own repo (5 to ~500 souls). Avoid: generic Halloween, cute or cartoonish ghosts.

## Direction contract

THESIS: A night walk through a Japanese hakaba where every deleted file has its own family grave. Refuses the western rounded-tombstone grid and the Halloween card wall.

OWN-WORLD: Aizome indigo night, granite sao-ishi pillars on stacked bases with the file name carved vertically, pale wooden sotoba slats behind, stone tōrō lanterns marking terraces, vermilion chōchin glow, hitodama from pale blue to violet, a washi reading strip, cause of death as a vermilion seal (横死 夭折 無縁 天寿). Shippori Mincho B1 throughout.

STORY: The visitor sees how many souls the repo holds and, from the flames alone, which deaths were restless. They walk terraces ordered by month of death, approach a grave, and read how it lived, how it died and its epitaph.

FIRST VIEWPORT: A hanging signboard with 供養, the repo name and the soul count between two chōchin; a one-line census by cause as seals; the first terrace of graves rising out of mist; the washi strip docked at the bottom inviting the visitor to approach a grave.

FORM: Brief-pinned by PLANO.md (hitodama, mist, incense, yūrei, lanterns, typed epitaph); no concept roll. Seed key: none (pinned). Code-led: no image generation available.

SIGNATURE: Approaching a grave (hover, focus or tap) lights its incense and brushes its epitaph onto the washi strip; violent graves raise a yūrei. Pillar height encodes lifespan, a tilted pillar marks violence, moss and cold incense mark the forgotten.

FINISH: unreviewed and undocumented is unfinished; this build ends with the finish review, the verdict, DESIGN.md, and every shipping raster carrying its provenance
